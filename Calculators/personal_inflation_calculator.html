How this is calculated

The method, in plain words

The government's figure. It comes from the Consumer Price Index published by the National Statistics Office, Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation. It shows how much prices in the latest month published are higher than in the same month a year earlier, for all of India, urban areas or rural areas, whichever you pick.

Divisions and weights. The index sorts everything households buy into 12 divisions. Each division's government weight is the share of an average household's spending that goes there, from the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey. Urban and rural households spend differently, so each has its own weights and its own price changes.

Your inflation. Your spend on a division divided by your total spend is your weight for it. Each division's price rise over the year is then counted by your weight instead of the government's. If your spending is split exactly like the government's, you get exactly the official figure. The published figures are rounded, so your figure is worked out as the official rate plus the difference between your basket and the government's basket, both calculated the same way.
