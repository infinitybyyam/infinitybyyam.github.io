How this is calculated

The method, in plain words

When money moves. The money goes in at the start of a month. The first withdrawal is taken at the end of that month, and one more at the end of every month after that. If you start withdrawing straight away, the money lasts slightly less than shown. The plan is taken to start in April, so each year of the plan is one tax year (April to March). For rupees this follows the Indian financial year; for other currencies the tax year is taken as the calendar year. Starting in another month moves some withdrawals into a different tax year, which changes the tax a little.

How it grows. The fund's yearly cost is taken off the yearly return, and what is left is turned into a true monthly rate, so twelve months of growth add up exactly to that yearly figure. The money still invested keeps growing while you withdraw.

The same return every year. Real returns jump around, and when you are withdrawing, the order matters: a fall early on, while most of the money is still invested, does far more damage than the same fall later. A steady return can make the money look like it lasts longer than a bumpy path with the same average. The "If the return is different" result shows how much the answer moves when the return is 2 points lower or higher every year.

Raising the withdrawal. The monthly amount goes up by your percentage at the start of each new year of the plan. At 0% it stays the same, and it buys less every year; the "last full withdrawal" line shows what it is worth by then.

Running out. If what is left is less than the next withdrawal, whatever is left is taken as a last part withdrawal and the plan stops. "Lasts" counts the full withdrawals.

Exit load. Charged on a withdrawal if the money has been held for no more than the months you set. It comes out of what you receive. Some funds let part of the holding go out free of load each year, so exit load shown here may be high.

Tax. Each withdrawal is treated as selling units. Its gain is what you receive minus what those units cost. If the money has been held for more than the long-term period, the gain is long-term; otherwise short-term. Gains are added up for each tax year (April to March), which here is each year of the plan. A short-term loss in a year is set off against long-term gain in the same year; a long-term loss is not set off against short-term gain. Losses are not carried to later years, so if losses happen, tax shown is, if anything, a little high. Long-term gain above the tax-free amount is taxed at the long-term rate, short-term gain at the short-term rate, and cess is added on top. The tax-free amount is used once in every tax year. Surcharge is not included, so tax shown is low for very high incomes. The basic exemption limit and any rebate are not applied, so for someone with little other income the real tax can be lower than shown, even zero. If you choose "Already invested", the profit in it is the money you start with minus what you originally put in, and its age is the years you enter. It is treated as one lot with one average cost and one age; real holdings bought at different times are sold oldest first, which usually means more tax early and less later than shown. Tax rules change, so always enter the rates that apply to you.

Who pays the tax. Each year's tax is paid at the end of the tax year out of the money you received. If you sell extra units to pay it instead, the money runs out sooner than shown.

Today's money. Each amount you receive, and each tax payment, is divided by inflation compounded up to the month it happens. It tells you what that money would buy at today's prices.

Money left at the end. If the money lasts the full period, the value left is shown, and also what you would keep if you sold it all at the end of the last month, after exit load and tax in that final tax year.

Largest monthly withdrawal. The highest starting amount, raised each year by your percentage, that leaves nothing at the end of the period without running out early. Exit load and tax do not change it, because they are paid from what you receive, not from the fund.

Currency. Choosing a currency changes only the symbol and how numbers are written. Nothing is converted, so enter every amount in the currency you choose. For rupees the tax settings start with example values; for other currencies they start at zero, because tax depends on where you live.
